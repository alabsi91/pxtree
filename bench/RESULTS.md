# pxtree benchmark results

How useful is pxtree's text output for finding layout bugs, compared with looking at a screenshot?

## Method

- Corpus: 14 pages in `bench/pages/` with 47 planted bugs across 13 kinds, plus 3 clean control pages. Each page has a `<name>.expect.json` that lists its runs and its bugs. A bug counts as found when one output line of its run contains every substring of its `match`.
- Noise: every summary line that matches no planted bug on that page. It is counted once per page even when several runs print it.
- Runs: each page is measured at 1280x800 light, plus 390x844, `--scheme dark`, `--scroll` or `--script` when a bug needs it. Every run uses `--no-diff`.
- Tokens are characters divided by 4. Wall time includes the Chromium launch.
- Screenshot baseline: before I opened any text output, I took viewport PNGs for every run with `node bench/run.mjs --screenshots`, looked at them, and wrote down in `bench/screenshot-baseline.json` which planted bugs I could see. The screenshots are viewport-sized, like pxtree's own `--screenshot`.
- Rerun: `npm run build && node bench/run.mjs`. It rewrites only the block between the generated markers below. Noise verdicts are read from `bench/noise-judgments.json`. A new noise line shows up as "not judged yet".

## Caveats

- Two planted bugs on the pricing page did not take effect at first. A more specific rule overrode the contrast color, and the centered card overflowed evenly on both sides without leaving the screen. I fixed both after viewing the screenshot and before any text scoring.
- The finding grammar changed while this benchmark ran. A parallel edit renamed `broken image` to `image not loaded`, `image distorted` to `image aspect N of natural`, `cousins misaligned` to `X tops a..b across siblings`, `uneven gaps` to `gaps ... between X` and `off-center-block` to `off center`. The first scoring pass used the old names and got 26 of 47 (55%). The expectations now use the current guide's names. Every amendment is listed under "Expectations amended" with its reason, including four naming corrections and two changes that made the nav expectations stricter.
- The test images are gradients with a white circle. A distorted image is easy to spot by eye because the circle turns into an ellipse, which flatters the screenshot on the image rows.
- I planted the bugs myself, so the screenshot verdicts are my best honest guess at what I would have reported without knowing. The notes say why for each one.
- The tables below come from the run after four rule fixes: sibling groups by majority classes, the CSS clip rule for absolute boxes, off center measured on border boxes, and "where it begins" for boxes that stick out of their own parent. The tag groups on a tree line are now written with no space between them.

Previous run, before those fixes: found 36 of 47 (77%), noise 1.4 per buggy page and 3.3 per clean page, median 524 tokens.

## Noise, judged

29 noise lines in total: 19 on the 14 buggy pages (1.4 per page) and 10 on the 3 clean pages (3.3 per page).

- true and useful: 8. Four repeat a planted bug on a second element. Two are planted bugs that the expectation names by another element: the dashboard overflow on `main`, and the pricing misalignment on `div.price`. Two are real bugs I did not plant: an 18px code overflow on the blog at 1280, and a marginal 4.4 contrast on the chat bubbles.
- true, expected carousel or marquee behaviour: 6. Every one had the context to dismiss it: `[role carousel]` or `[role marquee]`, `[clips 1 of 1 children]` on the clipper, `[translated x -N]` or `[animating]` on the track. Both carousels now group their hidden slides on one line (`×4` and `×3`).
- true but useless: 15. Seven are the visible carousel slide "overflowing" its translated track (3), intended ellipses (2) and backdrop coverage behind a div modal (2). One is the intended full-bleed hero on the hero page. Four are 1px of link padding cut by the header. Three are on clean pages and carry nothing to show they are deliberate: `overflows parent start and end 120: section.bleed` (full-bleed hero, no `[bleed]` tag printed), `overlaps img 12x40 ×4: div.avatars img` (avatar stack) and `19..28 wider than a ×2: nav.desktop-nav a` (text links are as wide as their words).
- wrong: 0. The earlier `past viewport end 750..1498 ×2: span.caption` is gone: the captions of hidden slides are now clipped by the carousel viewport, as CSS clips them.

The hidden off-canvas menu (`translateX(-100%)` with `visibility: hidden`) and the desktop nav hidden with `display: none` produced nothing. The decorative gradient blob produced nothing.

## Remaining misses and their cause

1. Off center finds 2 of 4. A label at the top of a 48px box with no vertical padding measures `text off center` 28 against a cap of 18. A glyph nudged 4px into the padding measures 8 against a cap of 3. Both sit past the `abs(d) <= 0.5 * (before + after)` cap, which is kept so one line at the top of a tall box does not fire.
2. The dashboard 390 overflow is on `main`. It is the deepest box that overflows its parent. The stats grid is what made it wide, but the grid fits inside main, so no measurement points at the grid.
3. The pricing misalignment is named by its first moving part, `div.price`, not the button. The rule reports once per row because later parts move with the first.
4. The 20px product buttons 4px apart pass WCAG 2.5.8 spacing, so no `small target` fires. The tool is right by its own standard.
