Why we moved our job queue to Postgres

By Dana Whitfield · 8 min read · March 3, 2026

For three years our background jobs ran on a dedicated message broker. It worked, but every incident review ended with the same sentence: the broker and the database disagreed about what had happened.

This post walks through how we replaced it with a table, a single index and SKIP LOCKED, and what we learned about throughput along the way.

The fastest queue is the one that commits in the same transaction as the work it describes.

The table

Everything starts with one table. Jobs are rows, workers claim rows, and a finished job is simply deleted.

CREATE TABLE jobs (id bigserial PRIMARY KEY, queue text NOT NULL, run_at timestamptz);
SELECT id FROM jobs WHERE queue = $1 ORDER BY run_at FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT 10;

Claiming ten rows at a time kept lock contention low even with forty workers polling the same queue.

Throughput

At peak we process about 1,800 jobs per second on a single primary. That is well below what a dedicated broker can do, and well above what we need.

If your volume is ten times ours, keep the broker. If it is not, the table is simpler to reason about, to back up and to debug at three in the morning.
